Project 1 · CS180 · Fall 2026

Images of the
Russian Empire

Sergei Prokudin-Gorskii photographed the Russian Empire in colour before colour film existed — three exposures per scene, through a red, a green and a blue filter. This aligns his glass plate negatives back into single colour photographs.

  • 17 plates aligned
  • ~2.2s per full-size scan
  • NumPy + Pillow

The problem

Three photos, one scene

Explain what the raw plate is and why you can't just stack the three thirds straight into an RGB image. Mention that the camera shifted between the three exposures, so each channel needs to be moved back into place.

Say what the search is actually looking for: a single (x, y) translation per channel, using blue as the fixed reference.

Part 1

Single-scale alignment

Describe the exhaustive search: every shift in a 31x31 window from -15 to +15, scoring each one and keeping the best.

Explain your two metrics in your own words. NCC (subtract the mean, normalise, take the dot product - so it ignores overall brightness and only compares pattern) and L2 / Euclidean distance. Say which you used as the default and why.

Explain why you crop the border before scoring. Two reasons worth mentioning: the plate edges are junk that looks identical in all three channels, and np.roll wraps pixels around to the opposite side.

Point out that NCC and L2 returned identical offsets on all three low-resolution images, and say what you think that means about how easy these three are.

Part 2

Coarse-to-fine pyramid

Explain why exhaustive search breaks down on the .tif files: the offsets run past 170 pixels, and each guess costs millions of pixel comparisons.

Walk through the pyramid. Shrink both channels repeatedly until the image is small, do the wide search there where it's cheap, then walk back up - doubling the offset at each step because the image doubles, and only re-checking a pixel or two around it.

Mention how you downsample (averaging 2x2 blocks rather than dropping pixels) and why blurring first matters.

State your search parameters and note they are fixed for every image - no per-image tuning.

Part 3

Three of my own

Say where these came from (the Library of Congress Prokudin-Gorskii archive) and why you picked these three.

Say how they did. All three aligned on the first try with the same fixed parameters. 01015 is worth a sentence - the plate is faded and speckled with damage, but the far shore still comes out sharp.

Where it breaks

The Emir of Bukhara

Explain why raw-pixel NCC fails here specifically. The key point: NCC on brightness assumes the three channels look alike, and on the Emir they don't - his robe is bright in one channel and dark in another, so the best brightness match is in the wrong place.

Note that this is the one failure the spec allows, and that the fix is in the next section.

Bells & whistles

Better features: matching on edges

Explain the idea: instead of comparing brightness, compare how fast brightness is *changing*. Edges sit in the same place in all three channels even when the brightness levels are completely different.

Describe your gradient briefly - a central difference horizontally and vertically, combined with Pythagoras to get steepness.

Important: say that you search on the edge maps but still roll the original channels, so the edges only guide the search and never appear in the output.
